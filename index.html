Not another agent.
The layer under yours.

Cottus doesn't decide what to do. It gives the agents you already trust a way to reach your desktop, and you a say in every step.

01

Your agent connects

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or any MCP client adds Cottus with one line, and sees every capability as a native tool.

claude mcp add cottus -- cottus mcp

02

Cottus checks every call

A small daemon on a per-user socket matches each call against your policy, asks you before anything sensitive, and appends it to the log under the agent's name.

03

It acts on your desktop

Apps through accessibility, by element id. Your own Chrome through an extension. Screenshots and real input only as a last resort, with the desktop's consent.

What agents can do today

Desktop apps
See a window's controls, press, fill fields and open apps, through accessibility. Ids like 3.12, not pixels.
Your browser
Work in your own Chrome, with your logins, by element id. A site you haven't allowed asks first.
Any other app
A screenshot, then real clicks and keys, after the desktop's own consent dialog.
Approvals
Anything sensitive opens a dialog naming the agent, the action and its arguments.
Watch mode
Super+Escape stops every agent. They wait while you type, and the control about to be used is outlined.
Dashboard
One local page: what's waiting for you, what's running, what was held back, and what each agent did.

You write the rules.

Allow, ask or deny, for any capability, pattern, website or app. Reading and reversible changes are allowed by default; anything sensitive asks.

An exact rule beats a pattern. A broken file stops the daemon instead of guessing. Password managers and the keyring are always off limits.

allowjust do it askshow me first denynever
~/.config/cottus/policy.toml
[rules]
"apps_press"     = "ask"     # confirm app clicks
"web_click"      = "ask"
"screen_*"       = "deny"    # no screenshots at all
"apps_snapshot"  = "allow"

[sites]
allow = ["github.com", "linear.app"]
deny  = ["mybank.example"]  # never read or act here

[deny_apps]
names = ["Signal"]         # never read or drive these

What Cottus will not do

  • Read your documents in passing. A snapshot shows how long a document is, never what it says. Reading it takes your approval.
  • Touch passwords or card numbers. Those fields are never read and never typed into. Cookies and browser storage aren't exposed at all.
  • Overwrite your work. Writing goes into single-line fields and empty text areas. Replacing a document's text asks first.
  • Let an agent approve itself. The dialog is drawn by your desktop. The dashboard can't approve a request or change the policy, on purpose.
  • Listen on the network. The daemon uses a socket only you can reach, and the dashboard answers on 127.0.0.1 alone.

Questions

Is Cottus an agent?

No. It doesn't plan or decide anything. It's the layer the agents you already use go through to reach your desktop: it checks each call against your policy, asks you when it matters, and keeps the record.

Which agents work with it?

Any MCP client. Claude Code, Codex and Cursor connect with one command each, and every call is logged under the agent's own name.

How do I stop it?

Press Super+Escape or run cottus stop. Nothing acts again until cottus resume, and the stop survives a restart. Agents also wait while you're typing.

Can an agent approve its own request?

No. Approvals come from a dialog your desktop draws, which an agent can't answer. Deny, closing it, or no answer within 60 seconds all refuse the action.

What if an app has no accessibility support?

Cottus falls back to a screenshot and real pointer and keyboard input. All of it is sensitive, so it asks you, and the first use needs the desktop's own consent.

When can I use it?

It's in early development. Desktop apps, the browser, approvals and the dashboard work today. Follow @CottusAI for the first release.
